Clear up the labels

## An assistant, an agent, and a teammate do not carry the same load

AI teammate is a useful buying phrase and an unsettled product category. Asana uses it for agents assigned inside projects. Lindy uses it for a Slack-centered work companion. Teammates.ai uses it for autonomous support, sales, and recruiting roles. The shared word does not create a shared operating model.

An assistant responds to a person and usually returns an answer, draft, or summary. An agent can plan steps, choose tools, and take action toward a goal. A teammate adds a working relationship around that agent behavior: a role, persistent context, a place where work arrives, recurring responsibilities, and a person who supervises the result.

AI employee is the stronger version of the teammate claim. It suggests standing ownership across work sessions and channels. Buyers should demand stronger evidence with the stronger label: a queue, schedule, memory, permissions, handoff, audit trail, and an explicit accountable owner.

- Assistant: useful for questions, drafts, summaries, and directed tasks.
- Agent: useful when the software must choose and execute several steps.
- Teammate: useful when the agent works inside a continuing team process.
- AI employee: useful when a persistent role owns a bounded body of recurring work.
- Notetaker: useful when the job begins and mostly ends with meeting capture.

**Do not buy the noun**

Ask where work enters, which systems can change, what persists tomorrow, and who approves the consequential step. Those four answers are the product.

Shortlist by fit

## Start with the workday you already have

A small service firm may need one teammate that was in the Zoom meeting, knows the inbox thread, and prepares the follow-up for approval. That maps to Workforce AI. A team that delegates in Slack and wants help across inbox, calendar, and meeting work should look closely at Lindy.

A founder producing content and campaigns may prefer Sintra's twelve packaged helpers. A company with a defined support, sales, or recruiting volume can evaluate Teammates.ai by role. A team whose operating record already lives in Asana gets more value from an Asana-native teammate than from another disconnected workspace.

Focused products should stay focused in the shortlist. Artisan is an outbound sales platform. Sembly is a meeting intelligence product. They can be excellent choices for those jobs without pretending to cover the rest of the company.

Relevance AI, Glean, and CrewAI expose more of the machinery. Relevance offers a visual workforce builder. Glean combines enterprise knowledge with governed actions. CrewAI gives builders a framework, visual editor, deployment path, and observability. Each needs a named owner with enough time and skill to keep it healthy.

Follow the channel

## Channel coverage decides what the teammate can actually own

A list of integrations can hide the real interface. An app connector may let an agent update a record without giving the owner a natural place to delegate. A Slack bot may be easy for the team to reach while having no role in a client meeting. A meeting agent may capture every decision and still stop when the call ends.

Separate owner channels, team channels, customer channels, and system actions. Workforce AI's SMS and WhatsApp access is for the verified owner speaking with the owner's AI employee. It is not a public customer line. Teammates.ai, by contrast, markets Raya and Adam for customer-facing support and sales channels. That is a different risk profile, not a box-checking advantage.

For every shortlisted product, draw one real job from arrival to finish. Mark the channel at each handoff. If the work leaves the product halfway through, count the copy, paste, re-brief, and manual send. The missing handoff is often where the owner's time returns to the process.

- Owner channel: where the owner can assign work or ask for status.
- Team channel: where coworkers can collaborate, review, or receive a handoff.
- Customer channel: where the system can communicate outside the company.
- System action: a read, create, update, send, publish, schedule, or delete in a connected tool.

Set the stopping point

## Approval belongs in the workflow, not in a promise

Human oversight ranges from a mandatory approval before every outside action to an escalation only when the model decides it is uncertain. A dashboard can display activity without stopping anything. A permission can block one tool while another remains open. Read the control itself.

Workforce AI keeps client-affecting work with the owner and drafts client-facing email for review. Lindy says outside-impact actions wait for approval. Artisan lets a campaign owner choose draft-and-wait or send. Relevance AI makes approval a setting on each connection. Glean administrators choose which write actions require confirmation. CrewAI provides human input and guardrail components, then leaves their implementation to the builder.

Teammates.ai is designed to act autonomously across customer service, sales, and recruiting within configured boundaries, with human escalation for exceptions. That may fit a high-volume operation. A relationship-led firm should begin with tighter review and widen authority only after representative work proves the policy.

- Require exact approval for messages, payments, public posts, commitments, and sensitive changes.
- Show the reviewer the destination, source facts, proposed change, and expected result.
- Stop on timeout instead of treating silence as consent.
- Log what the person approved and what the system actually did.
- Keep a visible pause control and test it before launch.

**The safest useful start**

Run read-only work first, then drafts, then exact approved actions. Autonomy should grow from accepted evidence, never from impatience with the approval queue.

Own month three

## The cheapest teammate may come with the largest management job

Subscription prices are not comparable until the billing unit is clear. Lindy, Sintra, CellCog, Teammates.ai, Relevance AI, Zapier, and Microsoft all meter work differently. Asana and Glean add the product to a wider platform relationship. CrewAI may move model, cloud, and engineering cost into your own stack.

Maintenance grows with choice. A role-based product needs current context, permissions, and review. A specialized teammate needs channel policy and escalation rules. A builder also needs workflow design, test cases, retries, deployment, observability, and somebody who understands the connected APIs.

Price one normal month and one ugly month. Include model or credit usage, seats, message or meeting volume, setup, review, corrections, and failed-run cleanup. Then divide the total by accepted outcomes. A draft nobody can use is not a cheap result.

- Low maintenance: connect, brief, review, and keep source context current.
- Medium maintenance: add schedules, channel policy, escalation rules, and usage monitoring.
- High maintenance: own tools, orchestration, tests, versions, budgets, and incident response.
- Every level still needs one accountable human owner.

Test the fit

## Use one scorecard for two weeks of real work

Select one job and twenty representative cases. Keep the source material, finish line, and scoring rule the same across products. Include an ordinary case, missing data, a duplicate, an exception, a sensitive request, and a failed connection.

Score accepted results, edit minutes, human interventions, correct escalations, missed escalations, elapsed time, and total usage. Record whether the product finished in the right system or left a person to carry the last mile.

Reject a platform when the required channel is missing, the review boundary cannot be enforced, or maintenance has no owner. Expand only when the accepted work stays useful under a busy-day sample. That is a calmer decision than picking the most impressive demo, and usually a cheaper one.

This guide compares published product design. Your pilot should compare your own accepted work. Keep those two kinds of evidence separate.

Questions

## What small-business buyers ask

What is an AI teammate?+

An AI teammate is software packaged to work inside a continuing team process. The useful version has a defined role, persistent context, an arrival channel for work, connected tools, visible handoffs, and a human owner. Vendors use the term differently, so inspect those mechanics instead of relying on the label.

What is the difference between an AI assistant, agent, teammate, and employee?+

An assistant responds to requests. An agent can plan and take tool-based steps. A teammate puts that behavior inside an ongoing collaboration. An AI employee adds standing responsibility across work sessions, channels, or schedules. These modes overlap, and one product may support several.

Which AI teammate is best for a non-technical owner?+

Choose a managed product that already works in the owner's real channels. Workforce AI fits supported Zoom, Gmail or Outlook drafts, dashboard chat, and private owner SMS and WhatsApp. Lindy fits Slack-centered work. Sintra offers ready-made role helpers. A builder platform is a poor bargain when nobody will maintain it.

Which AI teammate works across the most customer channels?+

Teammates.ai publishes the broadest customer-facing channel set in this comparison across its support and sales roles, including phone, email, chat, WhatsApp, social messaging, and LinkedIn. That breadth comes with a larger policy and supervision job. Workforce AI's SMS and WhatsApp channels are owner-only and should not be compared as customer support lines.

Which platform is best for meetings?+

Choose Sembly when the job is capture, search, structured notes, and meeting-derived output across several conferencing platforms. Choose Workforce AI when a live avatar must join a supported Zoom meeting and the context should continue into owner-side email and follow-up. Asana is useful when meeting output belongs in an existing project workflow.

Which AI teammate platforms let a person approve actions?+

Workforce AI keeps client-affecting work with the owner. Lindy holds outside-impact actions. Artisan offers campaign approval mode. Asana uses permissions and checkpoints. Relevance AI, Glean, Zapier, CellCog, and CrewAI provide configurable controls. Teammates.ai emphasizes autonomous work with escalation. Verify defaults and exact actions before connecting live channels.

How much do AI teammate platforms cost?+

Published entry points in this guide range from free to several hundred dollars per month, while Asana AI Teammates, Artisan, Glean, and enterprise editions require a quote or add-on. Include credits, actions, seats, model usage, meetings, messages, implementation, review, and corrections before comparing total cost.

How should a small business test an AI teammate?+

Use twenty cases from one repeated job over two weeks. Begin read-only, move to drafts, and test exact approved actions only after the earlier work passes. Measure accepted outcomes, edit time, interventions, escalation quality, elapsed time, and total usage. Keep live customer sends and irreversible actions out of the first comparison run.
